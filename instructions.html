Mobile setup

Get Atlas on your phone

The Android app is a remote client — it sends commands to Atlas running on your desktop over a private, encrypted network. Follow these steps once and you're set.

1
Desktop

Install Tailscale

Tailscale creates a private, encrypted tunnel between your phone and desktop — it works over WiFi, cellular, or anywhere else, without exposing anything to the open internet.

curl -fsSL https://tailscale.com/install.sh | sh
sudo tailscale up

Once connected, note your desktop's Tailscale IP — you'll need it later:

tailscale ip -4
2
Phone

Install Tailscale on your phone

Install Tailscale from the Play Store (or App Store on iOS) and sign in using the same account you used on the desktop. Both devices need to be on the same Tailscale network to talk to each other. Tailscale must be running on your phone prior to connecting every time.

3
Desktop

Generate your pairing QR code

With Atlas running on your computer, click the ⚙ settings icon in the app (between Cancel and Mute) to open the settings panel, then click:

Generate Mobile QR Code

A QR code will appear in the panel — it encodes your Atlas API key. This is what lets the mobile app authenticate with your desktop without you typing a long key by hand.

Keep this private — anyone who scans this QR code can control Atlas on your machine. Don't screenshot or share it.
4
Phone

Install the Atlas mobile app and scan

Install the Atlas Android app, then open it and choose Pair with Desktop (or the equivalent option on first launch). Point your camera at the QR code from Step 3 — the app will read the API key automatically.

5
Phone

Log in with your Linux password

The first time you connect, you'll be prompted to enter the login password for your desktop's Linux user account. This authenticates the connection over the network — it's the same password you use to log into your computer.

This is only asked once per device pairing — after that, the app reconnects automatically whenever both devices are on the Tailscale network.
✓

You're set

With both devices connected over Tailscale and paired, you can talk to Atlas from your phone — it processes everything on your desktop and sends the response back to your phone's mic and speaker.

Desktop needs to be powered on and Atlas running for the mobile app to work — it's a remote control, not a standalone assistant.
